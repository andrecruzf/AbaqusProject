\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) are widely used to assess the onset of localized
necking in sheet-metal forming. Their experimental identification and numerical
prediction are sensitive to the test setup, specimen geometry, sheet thickness,
material behaviour, and data-evaluation methodology.

This semester project contributes to the broader project \textit{Towards Robust
Forming Limit Curve Identification: Experiments and Modeling}. The report is
organised around the three connected project tasks. Task~I defines the experimental
campaign on the high-throughput Nakazima/Marciniak test bench, including
specimen-size, sheet-thickness, punch-radius variations.
Task~II provides the modular FLC identification framework used to evaluate
experimental and numerical strain data with multiple criteria. Task~III
develops the numerical simulation framework that automates the creation,
execution, and post-processing of the corresponding specimen and punch
configurations.

The implemented numerical framework generates Nakazima, Marciniak, and
Punch-in-Punch models in \software{Abaqus/Explicit}, submits simulations to the
ETH Euler cluster, and processes the resulting output databases without manual
intervention. 
Special attention is given to comparability between configurations. Specimen
width, sheet thickness, punch radius, forming travel, mass scaling, and mesh
controls are exposed as parameters, while the mesh generation workflow is
designed to maintain identical element sizes in the relevant observation zone.
A mesh-size modification script and two independent scalar sensitivity sweeps,
one for mesh refinement and one for mass scaling, provide the basis for
selecting production settings.

The post-processing pipeline extracts punch force--displacement histories,
energy histories, strain paths, and diagnostic
visualisations. The resulting data products are structured for comparison
between experimental and numerical test configurations and for use with the
modular FLC identification framework, including Volk--Hora, Merklein,
Pham--Sigvant, and Min--Stoughton criteria. The available results are presented
as experimental observations, preliminary criterion assessment, and
numerical-framework verification rather than as a completed production numerical
FLC campaign.
