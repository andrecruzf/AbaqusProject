\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) are widely used to assess the onset of necking and
fracture in sheet metal forming. Conventionally, FLCs are obtained from
Nakazima or Marciniak experiments by testing a series of specimen widths and
evaluating the strain field shortly before failure. Although this procedure is
well established, it remains time consuming and sensitive to specimen geometry,
test configuration, and the selected post-processing criterion.

This thesis develops an automated finite element simulation framework for the
numerical determination of forming limits. The framework generates Nakazima,
Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch models in Abaqus/Explicit, submits the
simulations to the ETH Euler cluster, and post-processes the resulting output
databases without manual intervention. A VUMAT implementation of the
Hosford-Coulomb fracture criterion is used to model failure. Seven specimen
widths, $W \in \{20,50,80,90,100,120,200\}$\,mm, are considered in order to
cover strain states from uniaxial tension to equibiaxial stretching.

The post-processing pipeline extracts punch force--displacement histories,
energy histories, forming-limit points, strain paths, and diagnostic
visualisations. The fracture frame is detected from the punch force drop and
classified using element deletion within the ISO-compliant dome zone. The
resulting data products are structured for direct comparison between test
configurations and for subsequent use in FLC identification criteria.

The work provides a reproducible numerical basis for parametric studies of
sheet thickness, material orientation, mesh density, mass scaling, and test
configuration. It also establishes a practical link between automated FE model
generation and the experimental FLC identification workflow used in the broader
project.
