\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) are widely used to assess the onset of localized
necking in sheet metal forming, but their identification remains sensitive to
test configuration, specimen geometry, sheet thickness, material behaviour, and
the evaluation criterion. This semester project contributes to the broader
project \textit{Towards Robust Forming Limit Curve Identification: Experiments
and Modeling} by connecting experimental analysis, criterion evaluation, and
automated numerical simulation.

The experimental part analyses an AA5754 Nakazima campaign across specimen widths, sheet thicknesses, punch diameters, and material orientations. The forming limits measured in the rolling and transverse directions remain within the observed repeat scatter, indicating that material anisotropy has no measurable influence on the onset of localized necking for the investigated material. In contrast, the punch-diameter study reveals systematic inconsistencies when the standard ISO~12004-2 fitting window is applied to nonstandard punch geometries. For some configurations, an entire FLC is shifted below the expected ordering, suggesting that the deviation is linked to the evaluation procedure rather than to isolated experimental scatter. The Volk–Hora results show broadly similar qualitative trends, but the departures from the expected ordering are limited to individual points rather than complete curves. The systematic ordering of the curves is consistent with the expected punch-diameter effect. This tendency is partially confirmed by the Volk–Hora results and can also be recovered with the ISO method when a geometry-dependent factor is introduced in the fitting-window definition.

The identification framework part examines the integration of a Min and Stoughton curvature criterion. The method is shown to operate on laboratory DIC exports and to produce onset frames in the range of established criteria, but its sensitivity to matrix length, reference frame choice, and DIC coverage prevents its use as a calibrated production criterion at this stage.

The numerical part develops a parameterised \software{Abaqus/Explicit} pipeline for Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch simulations. The framework automates model generation, Euler cluster submission, ODB processing, and structured result synchronisation. It exposes specimen width, sheet thickness, punch geometry, punch travel, mass scaling, and mesh controls as parameters, while maintaining comparable output formats for later experimental and numerical comparison. Initial mass scaling and mesh refinement sweeps on a Nakazima \texttt{W100} reference case define provisional production settings, but the numerical results are presented as framework verification rather than as a completed predictive FLC campaign.
