\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) are widely used to assess the onset of necking and
fracture in sheet metal forming. Their experimental and numerical
identification is sensitive to the test setup, specimen geometry, sheet
thickness, material behaviour, and the data evaluation methodology. These
sensitivities become especially relevant when FLCs are investigated with
smaller specimen dimensions, high-throughput testing, automated specimen
handling, and digital image correlation.

This thesis contributes to the project \textit{Towards Robust Forming Limit
Curve Identification: Experiments and Modeling}. The report follows the full
three-task structure of the project. Task~I defines the experimental campaign
on the high-throughput Nakazima/Marciniak test bench, including specimen-size,
sheet-thickness, punch-radius, and Punch-in-Punch variations. Task~II provides
the modular FLC identification framework used to evaluate experimental and
numerical strain data with multiple criteria. Task~III develops the numerical
simulation framework that automates the creation, execution, and
post-processing of the corresponding specimen and punch configurations.

The implemented numerical framework generates Nakazima, Marciniak, and
Punch-in-Punch models in Abaqus/Explicit, submits simulations to the ETH Euler
cluster, and processes the resulting output databases without manual
intervention. A VUMAT implementation of the Hosford-Coulomb fracture criterion
is used to represent failure.

Special attention is given to comparability between configurations. Specimen
width, sheet thickness, punch radius, forming travel, mass scaling, and mesh
controls are exposed as parameters, while the mesh generation workflow is
designed to maintain identical element sizes in the relevant observation zone.
A mesh-size modification script and two independent scalar sensitivity sweeps,
one for mesh refinement and one for mass scaling, provide the basis for
selecting production settings.

The post-processing pipeline extracts punch force--displacement histories,
energy histories, forming-limit points, strain paths, and diagnostic
visualisations. The resulting data products are structured for comparison
between experimental and numerical test configurations and for use with the
modular FLC identification framework, including Volk-Hora, Merklein,
Pham-Sigvant, and Stoughton-type criteria.
