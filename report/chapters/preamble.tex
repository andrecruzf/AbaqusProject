\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) assess localized necking in sheet metal forming,
but their identification depends on geometry, thickness, material behaviour,
and criterion. This project connects experimental Nakazima analysis, criterion
evaluation, and an automated numerical framework.

The AA5754 Nakazima campaign contains \num{280} tests with two repetitions per
configuration. It covers sheet thicknesses of \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter},
\SI{1.5}{\milli\meter}, \SI{2.0}{\milli\meter}, and
\SI{3.0}{\milli\meter}, punch diameters of \SI{40}{\milli\meter},
\SI{60}{\milli\meter}, \SI{80}{\milli\meter}, and
\SI{100}{\milli\meter}, specimen widths, and material orientations. Rolling
and transverse direction limits remain within repeat scatter, so no major
anisotropy effect is identified. In the punch diameter study, the standard
ISO~12004-2 fitting window can move complete FLCs out of the expected ordering
for nonstandard punch geometries. The Volk Hora comparison shows related
qualitative trends, but its ordering exceptions are limited to individual
points rather than whole curves. This motivates replacing the fixed ISO window
constant with the geometry dependent factor $D/10$, which recovers a more
intuitive ordering across punch diameters. Possible punch radius effects should
therefore be considered rather than treated as confirmed.

The identification framework integrates a Min and Stoughton curvature
criterion and applies it to the \num{56} DIC samples of the rolling direction,
\SI{100}{\milli\meter} punch subset. With one standard parameter set, it
detects 12 to 14 of 14 samples per thickness, with mean major strain
differences to ISO~12004-2 of \num{0.014} to \num{0.046}. Thickness dependent
recalibration improves the combined forming limit diagram distance to
\num{0.018} to \num{0.036} while keeping at least 13 of 14 detections, but
sensitivity to matrix length, reference frame, and DIC coverage remains.

The numerical part implements an automated \software{Abaqus/Explicit} workflow
for Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch in Punch simulations, including model
generation, Euler submission, ODB post processing, and result synchronisation.
For the \texttt{W100} reference case, the accepted setting is a mass scaling
target of \SI{1e-6}{\second} with $f_\mathrm{MR}=2$, giving a maximum kinetic
to internal energy ratio of \SI{1.8}{\percent}. Mesh convergence remains open
because $f_\mathrm{MR}=1$ is not quasistatic and $f_\mathrm{MR}=2$ and $3$ do
not define a reliable trend. Intermediate refinements such as
$f_\mathrm{MR}=1.25$ and $1.5$ are therefore needed before predictive FLC use.
