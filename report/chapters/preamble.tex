\chapter*{Abstract}

Forming Limit Curves (FLCs) assess localized necking in sheet metal forming,
but their identification depends on geometry, thickness, material behaviour,
and criterion. This project connects experimental Nakazima analysis, criterion
evaluation, and an automated numerical framework.

The AA5754 Nakazima campaign contains \num{280} tests with two repetitions per
configuration. It covers sheet thicknesses of \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter},
\SI{1.5}{\milli\meter}, \SI{2.0}{\milli\meter}, and
\SI{3.0}{\milli\meter}, punch diameters of \SI{40}{\milli\meter},
\SI{60}{\milli\meter}, \SI{80}{\milli\meter}, and
\SI{100}{\milli\meter}, specimen widths, and material orientations. Rolling
and transverse direction limits remain within repeat scatter, so no major
anisotropy effect is identified. In the punch diameter study, the standard
ISO~12004-2 fitting window can move complete FLCs out of the expected ordering
for nonstandard punch geometries. This motivates replacing the fixed ISO
window constant with the geometry dependent factor $D/10$, which recovers a
more intuitive ordering across punch diameters. Possible punch radius effects
should therefore be considered rather than treated as confirmed.

The identification framework integrates a Min and Stoughton curvature
criterion and applies it to the \num{56} DIC samples of the rolling direction,
\SI{100}{\milli\meter} punch subset. With one baseline parameter set, it
detects 12 to 14 of 14 samples per thickness, with mean
$|\Delta\varepsilon_1|$ values to ISO~12004-2 of \num{0.014} to \num{0.046}
and mean $|\Delta\varepsilon_2|$ values of \num{0.007} to \num{0.016}.
A thickness dependent sweep indicates parameter sensitivity, with separate
detection and ISO-match rankings reported through
$|\Delta\varepsilon_1|$ and $|\Delta\varepsilon_2|$.

The numerical part implements an automated \software{Abaqus/Explicit} workflow
for Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch in Punch simulations, including model
generation, Euler submission, ODB post processing, and result synchronisation.
For the \texttt{W100} reference case, the working setting is a mass scaling
target of \SI{1e-6}{\second} with $f_\mathrm{MR}=1.41$, which offers the best
compromise between computation time and accuracy while remaining quasi
static by the energy criterion. A friction
check showed that only frictionless punch contact keeps the fracture
dome-centred, which supports the frictionless baseline for the lubricated
experiments. The fracture-based extraction already yields an exploratory
fracture forming limit curve, and a necking-based FLC awaits a robust onset
detection.
