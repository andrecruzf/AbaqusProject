\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Sheet metal forming is a fundamental manufacturing process in the automotive,
aerospace, and consumer-goods industries. A key challenge in forming operations
is predicting the onset of localised necking and fracture, since this limit
determines whether a blank can be formed into a target geometry without
failure. The \textit{Forming Limit Diagram} (FLD) is the standard engineering
representation of this limit. It maps the major and minor principal strains,
$(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$, at the onset of instability and defines the
Forming Limit Curve (FLC) as the boundary between safe deformation and failure.

Despite its broad industrial acceptance, the experimental determination of
FLCs is sensitive to the test setup, specimen geometry, sheet thickness,
material behaviour, and the criterion used to detect necking
. Two standardised test configurations are commonly used: the
Nakazima test, which deforms the specimen with a hemispherical punch, and the
Marciniak test, which uses a flat cylindrical punch. Both are described in
ISO12004-2\cite{ISO120042}. The Marciniak configuration generally produces
more linear strain paths than the Nakazima configuration, while the latter
remains attractive because of its simpler specimen and tooling requirements.

The Nakazima test is a long-established method for the experimental
determination of forming limits and remains widely used in research and
industrial practice. Nevertheless, the accurate identification of the onset of
localised necking remains an active topic of investigation. Examples include
time-dependent approaches such as the Volk–Hora criterion
\cite{VolkHora2011} and spatio-temporal curvature-based approaches such as the
Min–Stoughton method~\cite{Min2017}.

Numerical simulation provides a complementary approach to physical testing.
Once a calibrated material model is available, finite-element simulations can
be used to vary specimen geometry, sheet thickness, punch radius, material
orientation, and process parameters without manufacturing additional samples.
This makes simulation particularly useful for studying size effects and for
generating consistent data for the evaluation of different FLC identification
criteria.

\section{Project Context}

This work is part of the project \textit{Towards Robust Forming Limit Curve
Identification: Experiments and Modeling}. The project outline starts from the
need for reliable sheet metal formability data and from the observation that
FLC identification is sensitive to test setup, specimen geometry, sheet
thickness, material behaviour, and evaluation methodology. It combines high
throughput testing, DIC strain measurements, identification criteria, and
numerical simulation to make forming limit assessment more robust and
reproducible.

The broader project is organised around three tasks. The present report covers
only the parts of those tasks that were available within this semester project:

\begin{description}
  \item[Task I] The experimental work is restricted to the AA5754 Nakazima
  campaign. It analyses the available specimen widths, four sheet thicknesses,
  four hemispherical punch diameters, and the rolling and transverse material
  directions. Marciniak experiments, Punch in Punch experiments, additional
  materials, and the automated specimen loading system are part of the broader
  project scope but are not experimentally assessed here.
  \item[Task II] The identification work uses the available FLC workflow to
  evaluate ISO~12004-2 and Volk--Hora results, with emphasis on the sensitivity
  of the ISO fitting window for nonstandard punch diameters. The additional
  criterion studied in detail is the Min--Stoughton curvature method. It is
  assessed on the rolling direction, \SI{100}{\milli\meter} punch subset rather
  than delivered as a fully calibrated production criterion.
  \item[Task III] The numerical work develops a parameterised
  \software{Abaqus/Explicit} workflow for Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch in
  Punch model generation, Euler execution, and post processing. The numerical
  results in this report are limited to framework verification and scalar
  mass scaling, mesh refinement, and friction checks. They are not yet a
  completed predictive numerical FLC campaign.
\end{description}

\section{Objectives}

The objectives of this semester project follow the three tasks defined in the
project outline: the generation and analysis of forming-limit experiments, the
extension of the FLC identification framework, and the development of an
automated numerical simulation framework. In the present report, these
objectives are addressed through the AA5754 Nakazima campaign, the
Min--Stoughton criterion study, and the \software{Abaqus/Explicit} pipeline developed for
the broader Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch scope.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Perform and analyse the Nakazima experimental campaign by crossing the
  available sheet thicknesses with the hemispherical punch diameters. The
  analysis focuses on the influence of sheet thickness, punch diameter,
  specimen width, and material orientation on the experimentally determined
  forming-limit curves.
  \item Evaluate the resulting digital image correlation (DIC) strain data with the existing FLC
  identification workflow and assess the sensitivity of the ISO~12004-2
  evaluation to non-standard punch diameters and fitting-window choices.
  \item Investigate the implementation of the Min--Stoughton curvature criterion
  as an extension of the identification framework, including the geometric
  interpretation, required \software{VIC-3D} data fields, and remaining integration
  challenges.
  \item Develop a parameterised \software{Abaqus/Explicit} framework that can generate,
  submit, and post-process Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch simulations
  from a common configuration interface.
  \item Implement mesh-size controls that preserve comparable element sizes
  across specimen and punch configurations, and define the mesh-refinement and
  mass-scaling studies required before quantitative production simulations are
  evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Report Structure}

Chapter~\ref{chap:theory} introduces the theoretical background needed for the
three project tasks. It summarises sheet-metal formability, forming-limit
diagrams, the Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch test concepts, and the
forming-limit identification methods considered in this work.

Chapter~\ref{chap:experimental_campaign} describes the AA5754 experimental
campaign, including the material batches, specimen widths, punch diameters,
sheet thicknesses, orientation cases, and DIC data acquisition. The reported
experimental results focus on the Nakazima subset of the broader campaign.

Chapter~\ref{chap:identification_framework} presents the existing FLC
identification workflow and the investigated Min--Stoughton extension. The
chapter clarifies which criteria were already available, what was studied for
the curvature-based method, and which integration issues remain open.

Chapter~\ref{chap:numerical_model} describes the automated \software{Abaqus/Explicit}
framework developed for Task~III. It covers the common configuration interface,
model generation for the three forming setups, mesh-size control, Euler cluster
execution, quasi-static solution strategy, and post-processing outputs.

Chapter~\ref{chap:results} reports the available results. The first part
analyses the experimental influence of material orientation, punch diameter,
sheet thickness, and ISO~12004-2 window choice. The second part reports the
preliminary Min--Stoughton sensitivity study. The third part documents the
current numerical framework status and defines the mass-scaling and
mesh-refinement studies required before production simulations are interpreted
quantitatively.

Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} summarises the main findings, and
Chapter~\ref{chap:future_work} lists the work required to complete the
experimental evaluation, Min--Stoughton integration, and numerical validation.
Appendix~\ref{app:pipeline} documents the simulation pipeline, configurable
parameters, output files, and cluster execution details.
