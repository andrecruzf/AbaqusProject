\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Sheet metal forming is widely used across the automotive, aerospace, and
consumer goods industries, for example in the manufacture of automotive
chassis and body panels. Predicting the onset of localised necking and
fracture during forming is essential, since this limit determines whether a
blank can be formed into a target geometry without failure. The
\textit{Forming Limit Diagram} (FLD) is the standard engineering
representation of this limit. It maps the major and minor principal strains,
$(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$, at the onset of instability and defines the
Forming Limit Curve (FLC) as the boundary between safe deformation and failure.

Despite its broad industrial acceptance, the experimental determination of
FLCs is sensitive to the test setup, specimen geometry, sheet thickness,
material behaviour, and the criterion used to detect necking. Two standardised
test configurations are commonly used: the Nakazima test, which deforms the
specimen with a hemispherical punch, and the Marciniak test, which uses a flat
cylindrical punch, both described in ISO~12004-2~\cite{ISO120042}. The
Marciniak configuration generally produces more linear strain paths than the
Nakazima configuration, while the latter remains attractive because of its
simpler specimen and tooling requirements.

The Nakazima test is an established method for the experimental
determination of forming limits and remains widely used in research and
industrial practice. Nevertheless, the accurate identification of the onset of
localised necking remains an active topic of investigation. Examples include
time-dependent approaches such as the Volk--Hora criterion~\cite{VolkHora2011}
and spatio-temporal curvature-based approaches such as the Min--Stoughton
method~\cite{Min2017}.

Once a calibrated material model is available, finite element simulations
complement physical testing by varying specimen geometry, sheet thickness,
punch radius, material orientation, and process parameters without
manufacturing additional samples. This makes simulation particularly useful
for studying size effects and for generating consistent data for the
evaluation of different FLC identification criteria.

\section{Objectives}

This semester project focuses on forming limit identification for AA5754 sheet
metal. It combines the available Nakazima experiments, the evaluation of FLC
identification methods, and the development of a parameterised
\software{Abaqus/Explicit} workflow for forming simulations. The experimental
analysis is restricted to the available Nakazima campaign, while the numerical
results are limited to framework verification and preliminary checks rather
than a completed predictive numerical FLC campaign.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Perform and analyse the Nakazima experimental campaign by crossing the
  available sheet thicknesses with the hemispherical punch diameters. The
  analysis focuses on the influence of sheet thickness, punch diameter,
  specimen width, and material orientation on the experimentally determined
  forming limit curves.
  \item Evaluate the resulting digital image correlation (DIC) strain data with the existing FLC
  identification workflow and assess the sensitivity of the ISO~12004-2
  evaluation to nonstandard punch diameters and fitting window choices.
  \item Investigate the implementation of the Min--Stoughton curvature criterion
  as an extension of the identification framework, including the geometric
  interpretation, required \software{VIC-3D} data fields, and remaining integration
  challenges.
  \item Develop a parameterised \software{Abaqus/Explicit} framework that can generate,
  submit, and post-process Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch simulations
  from a common configuration interface.
  \item Implement mesh size controls that preserve comparable element sizes
  across specimen and punch configurations, and define the mesh-refinement and
  mass-scaling studies required before quantitative production simulations are
  evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

Following the project outline, these objectives are grouped into three tasks.
Task~I covers the experimental campaign and its evaluation, Task~II the
Min--Stoughton criterion study, and Task~III the automated numerical
framework. Chapter~\ref{chap:results} is organised along these tasks.

\section{Report Structure}

Chapter~\ref{chap:theory} introduces the theoretical background needed for the
experimental, identification, and numerical parts of the report. It summarises
sheet metal formability, forming limit diagrams, the Nakazima, Marciniak, and
Punch-in-Punch test concepts, and the forming limit identification methods
considered in this work.

Chapter~\ref{chap:experimental_campaign} describes the AA5754 experimental
campaign, including the material batches, specimen widths, punch diameters,
sheet thicknesses, orientation cases, and DIC data acquisition. The reported
experimental results focus on the Nakazima subset of the broader campaign.

Chapter~\ref{chap:identification_framework} presents the existing FLC
identification workflow and the investigated Min--Stoughton extension. The
chapter clarifies which criteria were already available, what was studied for
the curvature based method, and which integration issues remain open.

Chapter~\ref{chap:numerical_model} describes the automated \software{Abaqus/Explicit}
framework developed for the forming simulations. It covers the common
configuration interface, model generation for the three forming setups, mesh
size control, Euler cluster execution, quasi-static solution strategy, and
post-processing outputs.

Chapter~\ref{chap:results} reports the available results. The first part
analyses the experimental influence of material orientation, punch diameter,
sheet thickness, and ISO~12004-2 window choice. The second part reports the
preliminary Min--Stoughton sensitivity study. The third part reports the
mass-scaling, mesh-refinement, and friction-sensitivity studies and fixes the
working numerical configuration for the future production campaign.

Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} summarises the main findings, and
Chapter~\ref{chap:future_work} lists the work required to complete the
experimental evaluation, Min--Stoughton integration, and numerical validation.
Appendix~\ref{app:pipeline} documents the simulation pipeline, configurable
parameters, output files, and cluster execution details.
