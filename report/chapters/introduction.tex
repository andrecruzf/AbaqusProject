\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Sheet metal forming is a fundamental manufacturing process in the automotive, aerospace, and consumer goods industries. A key challenge in forming operations is predicting the onset of localised necking and fracture, since this limit determines whether a blank can be formed into a target geometry without failure. The \textit{Forming Limit Diagram} (FLD) is the standard engineering representation of this limit. It maps the major and minor principal strains, $(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$, at the onset of instability and defines the Forming Limit Curve (FLC) as the boundary between safe deformation and failure.

Despite its broad industrial acceptance, the experimental determination of FLCs is sensitive to the test setup, specimen geometry, sheet thickness, material behaviour, and the criterion used to detect necking~\cite{ISO120042}. Two standardised test configurations are commonly used: the Nakazima test, which deforms the specimen with a hemispherical punch, and the Marciniak test, which uses a flat cylindrical punch. Both are described in ISO~12004-2~\cite{ISO120042}. The Marciniak configuration generally produces more linear strain paths than Nakazima, while Nakazima tests remain attractive because of their simpler specimen and tooling requirements.

Numerical simulation provides a complementary approach to physical testing. Once a calibrated material model is available, finite element simulations can be used to vary specimen geometry, sheet thickness, punch radius, material orientation, and process parameters without manufacturing additional samples. This makes simulation particularly useful for studying size effects and for generating consistent data for the evaluation of different FLC identification criteria.

\section{Project Context}

This work is part of the project \textit{Towards Robust Forming Limit Curve Identification: Experiments and Modeling}. The overall project is divided into three connected tasks:

\begin{description}
  \item[Task I] A large-scale experimental study on several engineering materials using a high-throughput Nakazima/Marciniak test bench. The campaign investigates size effects on FLCs across specimen geometries, sheet thicknesses, punch radii, and a novel Punch-in-Punch setup, while also assessing the robustness of the automated specimen loading system.
  \item[Task II] Development of a modular, robust, and user-friendly FLC identification framework in Matlab/Python. The framework integrates and improves established criteria such as Volk-Hora~\cite{VolkHora2011}, Merklein~\cite{Merklein2010}, and Pham-Sigvant~\cite{Pham2023}, addresses unstable ISO~12004 evaluations for materials affected by dynamic strain ageing, and includes a Stoughton-type criterion~\cite{Min2017}.
  \item[Task III] Development of a numerical simulation framework that fully automates model creation, execution, and post-processing for all specimen and punch configurations relevant to Task~I. A central requirement is maintaining identical element size across configurations and enabling an automated mesh-size study.
\end{description}

\section{Objectives}

The objective of this semester project is to connect the experimental,
identification, and numerical parts of the forming-limit workflow and to bring
the numerical pipeline to a reproducible state. The report does not aim to
deliver a complete validated numerical FLC database for every test
configuration. Instead, it documents the implemented framework, reports the
available experimental trends, and defines the numerical studies required before
production simulations can be interpreted quantitatively.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Analyse the available Nakazima experimental results, with emphasis on
  the influence of material orientation, punch diameter, sheet thickness, and
  the ISO~12004-2 evaluation window.
  \item Document the existing FLC identification framework and clarify the
  status of the implemented criteria and the investigated Min--Stoughton
  curvature extension.
  \item Develop a parameterised Abaqus/Explicit pipeline that can generate,
  submit, and post-process Nakazima, Marciniak, and Punch-in-Punch simulations
  from a common configuration interface.
  \item Implement mesh controls that preserve comparable element sizes across
  specimen and punch configurations and define the independent mesh-refinement
  and mass-scaling studies needed to select production settings.
  \item Export a consistent set of numerical result files, including force--
  displacement histories, energy histories, strain paths, forming-limit points,
  and diagnostic visualisations, so that future simulations can be compared
  directly with the experimental and identification workflows.
\end{enumerate}
