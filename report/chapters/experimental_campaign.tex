\chapter{Experimental Campaign}
\label{chap:experimental_campaign}

\section{Experimental Setup}

All experiments were conducted using the high-throughput forming test bench
available at the Institute of Virtual Manufacturing (IVP). The system combines
automated specimen handling with DIC measurements, enabling the efficient
acquisition of strain-field data for a large number of test configurations.
Figure~\ref{fig:test_bench} shows the test bench used for the experimental
campaign. For a detailed description of the machine's mechanical design, see
Groner~\cite{Groner2021}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{img/TestBench.jpg}
    \caption{High-throughput forming test bench used for the experimental campaign.}
    \label{fig:test_bench}
\end{figure}

Specimen preparation and lubrication follow the established procedure for this
bench~\cite{Sen2025}. Each blank is first cleaned with ethanol to remove dust
deposits accumulated during storage. A random black-and-white speckle pattern
is then applied by spray paint for the DIC measurement, as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:speckle_ptfe}. To reduce punch--blank friction, so that the measured
strains reflect the pure forming mechanism rather than frictional effects, the
punch side of each blank is covered with a self-adhesive PTFE film, onto which
a thin layer of lanolin grease is applied. The blank is clamped along its
outer edge before the test starts, and each test then drives the punch into the
clamped blank at constant speed until a sudden drop in punch force marks
fracture and terminates the run. Table~\ref{tab:test_conditions} summarises the
key test conditions common to all specimens in the campaign.

\section{Material}

All specimens were cut from AA5754 aluminium alloy sheets supplied by
\textit{Constellium}. Four sheet thicknesses were delivered, each identified by an
internal code composed of the delivery date and a letter suffix encoding the
thickness. The sheet dimensions are given with respect to the rolling direction
(RD) and transverse direction (TD) of the material, which are relevant for the
specimen orientation during testing. Table~\ref{tab:material_delivery}
summarises the delivered batches.

\section{Specimen Geometries and Test Configurations}

The experimental campaign employs Nakazima tests with hemispherical punches. By
varying the specimen width, strain paths ranging from uniaxial tension to
equi-biaxial stretching are generated, enabling the construction of complete
FLDs. Seven standard specimen widths are used: \texttt{W020}, \texttt{W050},
\texttt{W080}, \texttt{W090}, \texttt{W100}, \texttt{W120}, and \texttt{W200}.
Narrow specimens promote uniaxial deformation, whereas wider specimens
progressively shift the strain state towards biaxial stretching.
Figure~\ref{fig:specimen_geometries} shows the full geometry set.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{img/Sen2025_specimen_geometries.png}
    \caption[Specimen geometry set]{Specimen geometry set used with the automated
    testing bench, with the grip ears and pin holes required for automated
    handling and positioning. From Sen~\cite{Sen2025}.}
    \label{fig:specimen_geometries}
\end{figure}

The experimental results reported in this work therefore focus on the Nakazima
subset of the broader project, while Marciniak and Punch-in-Punch configurations
are retained as numerical framework capabilities and future extension cases.

\section{Data Acquisition}

DIC was used to measure the full-field strain evolution throughout each test.
Two synchronised cameras mounted above the press record the deforming specimen
at the acquisition and storage rates given in Table~\ref{tab:test_conditions}.
The fracture frame is identified automatically from the force drop and a
neural-network crack detector applied to the last recorded frames~\cite{Sen2025}.
The recorded strain histories constitute the primary input for the
forming-limit identification procedures presented in
Chapter~\ref{chap:identification_framework}. To facilitate comparison with
numerical simulations, both experimental and numerical datasets are organised
around the same physical quantities, including major and minor strain histories,
force--displacement responses, fracture location, and forming-limit points
within the FLD. The experimental forming limits obtained from this campaign are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:results_task1}.
