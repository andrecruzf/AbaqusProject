\chapter{Experimental Campaign}
\label{chap:experimental_campaign}

\section{Overview}

The experimental campaign constitutes the foundation of the present work and
aims to generate a comprehensive dataset for the investigation of forming limit
curves (FLCs). Particular emphasis is placed on assessing the influence of
specimen geometry, sheet thickness, punch geometry, material orientation, and
loading path on the experimentally determined forming limits. The resulting
dataset serves both for the evaluation of forming-limit identification methods
and for the validation of the numerical simulation framework developed within
this project.

\section{Experimental Setup}

All experiments were conducted using the high-throughput forming test bench
available at the Institute of Virtual Manufacturing (IVP). The system combines
automated specimen handling with DIC measurements, enabling the efficient
acquisition of strain-field data for a large number of test configurations.
Figure~\ref{fig:test_bench} shows the test bench used for the experimental
campaign.

Constellium. Four sheet thicknesses were delivered, each identified by an
internal code composed of the delivery date and a letter suffix encoding the
thickness. The sheet dimensions are given with respect to the rolling direction
(RD) and transverse direction (TD) of the material, which are relevant for the
specimen orientation during testing. Table~\ref{tab:material_delivery}

\section{Specimen Geometries and Test Configurations}

The experimental campaign employs Nakazima tests with hemispherical punches. By
varying the specimen width, strain paths ranging from uniaxial tension to
equi-biaxial stretching are generated, enabling the construction of complete
FLDs. Seven standard specimen widths are used: W20, W50, W80, W90, W100, W120,
and W200. Narrow specimens promote uniaxial deformation, whereas wider specimens
progressively shift the strain state towards biaxial stretching.

The experimental results reported in this work therefore focus on the Nakazima
subset of the broader project, while Marciniak and Punch-in-Punch configurations
are retained as numerical framework capabilities and future extension cases.

\section{Data Acquisition}

DIC was used to measure the full-field strain evolution throughout each test.
The recorded strain histories constitute the primary input for the
forming-limit identification procedures presented in
Chapter~\ref{chap:identification_framework}. To facilitate comparison with
numerical simulations, both experimental and numerical datasets are organised
around the same physical quantities, including major and minor strain histories,
force--displacement responses, fracture location, and forming-limit points
within the FLD. The experimental forming limits obtained from this campaign are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:results_task1}.
