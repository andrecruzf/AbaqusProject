\chapter{Experimental Campaign}\label{chap:experimental_campaign}

\section{Role in the Project}

Task~I of the project provides the experimental basis for robust forming-limit
identification. Its objective is to generate FLC data on a high-throughput
forming test bench while deliberately varying the parameters that can influence
the measured limit curve: specimen geometry, sheet thickness, punch radius,
material, and test configuration. The numerical framework and identification
criteria described in later chapters are designed around this experimental
scope.

\section{Test Configurations}

The campaign is centred on Nakazima and Marciniak tests, which are the standard
experimental routes for FLC determination. By changing specimen width, the
strain state is varied from uniaxial tension through plane strain to biaxial
stretching. The project also includes a Punch-in-Punch configuration, where a
two-stage punch motion is used to control the strain path in the dome zone.

The parameters of interest are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item specimen width and geometry;
  \item sheet thickness;
  \item punch radius and punch travel;
  \item material and material orientation;
  \item test configuration: Nakazima, Marciniak, or Punch-in-Punch.
\end{itemize}

\section{Automation and Robustness}

The high-throughput test bench and automated specimen loading system are
important parts of the project because they determine whether FLC measurements
can be repeated with low operator dependence. Robustness is therefore evaluated
not only through the final FLC values, but also through the consistency of the
test setup, crack location, strain-field quality, and data availability for
post-processing.

Digital image correlation provides the strain-field histories used by the Task~II
identification framework. To make experimental and numerical results comparable,
both data streams are organised around the same physical quantities: major and
minor strain histories, force--displacement response, crack or endpoint frame,
and forming-limit points in FLD space.
